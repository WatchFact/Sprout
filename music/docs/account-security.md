# Sprout accounts and security

Application authentication is independent of OpenAI Sites hosting access. `/register`, `/login`, `/recover` and `/api/auth` use Sprout email addresses and passwords. The native hosting gate and its reserved routes remain intact; native headers never sign in to a Sprout account. `getHostingUser` is used only for explicit one-time import/owner bootstrap and abuse limiting. All app authorization reads `getSproutPrincipal`/`getSproutUser` from the server-only `lib/sprout-auth.ts`.

Passwords use a unique 128-bit salt, PBKDF2-SHA256 at the Workers-compatible 100,000-iteration cap, followed by HMAC-SHA256 with the configured `AUTH_PASSWORD_PEPPER` secret. Plaintext passwords are never stored. Preserve this secret across deployments; changing it invalidates stored password hashes. Use long unique passphrases (15–128 characters). Password recovery uses a 256-bit random, hashed, single-use rotating key. It does not bypass enabled TOTP. Changing passwords or recovery keys revokes sessions. Hashed random Sprout sessions expire after seven days; cookies are HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax and host-only.

The first explicit import per native hosting identity claims its old channel records atomically without rekeying or deleting media. An imported parent retains its existing brand channels and enabled 2FA. Owner authority is granted only during this bootstrap from the trusted native moderator-email configuration. Choosing an owner email in a registration form never grants authority. Contact email is not an authentication or authorization claim.

Parent accounts can publish personally, own brand channels, or opt into viewing-only mode. Viewing-only parents cannot publish comments, edit public profiles or upload media. Existing personal uploads/comments must be removed before switching to viewing-only; existing content is never silently deleted. These parents share their private viewing library across their brand channels. Normal personal publishing accounts retain channel-specific libraries. Brand selection is checked against ownership on every request and cannot change parent authentication or 2FA identity.

Sprout TOTP is RFC 6238 SHA-1, six digits, 30 seconds, with a one-step tolerance, atomic replay prevention and five attempts per minute. Ten hashed recovery codes are consumed atomically. Authenticator secrets are AES-GCM encrypted with parent-bound additional data and the configured `TWO_FACTOR_ENCRYPTION_KEY`; preserve that key. Second-factor sessions expire after twelve hours. Ordinary data responses/exports exclude credentials, recovery hashes, challenges and security sessions. Sign-out revokes the Sprout session and clears app cookies without signing out of the hosting provider.

Email/source throttles limit login, registration, password changes and recovery. Source identifiers are short-lived hashes; raw request IPs are not stored in D1. Login failures are generic, and unknown email addresses perform password hashing. Session and import tokens are never accepted as user-supplied roles. Parent suspension revokes its sessions and covers all children. Site-owner administration whitelists account/channel metadata and never returns hashes, email addresses or recovery secrets.

Email verification is implemented but delivery is disabled until `RESEND_API_KEY` and `VERIFICATION_EMAIL_FROM` are configured. Email verification, creator badges and 2FA remain distinct. Video and Music are separate Sites with separate account databases and host-only cookies.

Run `node tests/sprout-auth.cjs` and `node tests/account-security.cjs`. Tests use SQLite, native crypto, mocked request identity/cookies/provider delivery and React SSR. Browser visual QA and live mail delivery are not included.

Email sign-in normalizes case and surrounding whitespace. Profile names remain separate public labels. New accounts and email changes atomically reserve an email in private `auth-email` records and reject reused addresses, including addresses on older accounts. Existing account IDs, channels, passwords and 2FA are preserved. Legacy duplicate-email accounts authenticate by password; matching more than one displays an account picker only after password verification. Recovery finds the matching email/account recovery key and never merges account data.
